# Afterpay Merchant Brand Guidelines (US) — YummyColours

## 1. Snapshot
- **Subject:** A 40-slide 16:9 deck (1152 × 648 pt, rendered at 1400 × 787 px), dated 27 October 2020. It is addressed to US *merchants*: how to show Afterpay on their sites, checkouts, social posts and email.
- **Why it's remarkable:** The whole identity runs on one hex, Bondi Mint #B2FCE4, plus black and white. The deck spends more than half its pages on real merchant placements (top strip, PDP, cart, checkout, lightbox, email), with concrete sizes and legal copy. It is a partner kit more than a manifesto.

## 2. Composition & layout
- **Uniform slide template.** A header "AFTERPAY MERCHANT BRAND GUIDELINES" (≈8 px bold caps) sits at x=44, y=34 px, with the folio top-right. Slide titles are two-line Demibold at about 34 px (≈28 pt), at y≈75–150. Body copy is about 12–16 px. The confidentiality footer is at y≈752. Outer margin is 44 px (≈36 pt) on all sides.
- **Grounds:**
  - Spec pages use an off-white #F1F1F1 (56–83% coverage), not pure white. White is reserved for logo tiles and the right half of p23.
  - Mission, brand and logo-meaning pages flood mint (#B2FCE3, 84–87%).
  - The cover and chapter dividers (p1, p12, p22, p40) are black (91%), with a mint title at the bottom right.
- **Values pages (p6–9)** are full-bleed lifestyle photos with a white headline of about 110 px ("Be Brave") at the left third, plus three short lines of white body text. There is no scrim, so body text sits directly over busy water and foliage.
- **Application pages** use a left text column (~33%) and right-hand wireframes or mock-ups. p26 maps each strip and banner to its position on a schematic web page with blue leader lines.

## 3. Typography
- **Embedded fonts (pdffonts):** Italian Plate No2 Expanded (Regular, Italic, Demibold, Bold) and Italian Plate No1 Expanded (Regular, Extrabold) (both Displaay Type Foundry grotesks); plus Proxima Nova (Regular → Extrabold), which appears only inside the website and checkout mock-ups.
- **Stated rule (p20).** The primary face is Italian Plate No2 Expanded, described as a sans with "soft angles". Its styles are Regular, Regular Italic, Demibold and Bold. Two rules: "Do not use all caps", and merchants should use *their own* brand typeface next to Afterpay assets. That second rule is pragmatic for a partner kit.
- **Observed scale on the slides:** display ~110 px (values, messaging), title ~34 px Demibold, lead ~24 px Regular (p23), body ~12 px. The deck publishes no sizes, leading or tracking.
- The wordmark is lowercase "afterpay" in a rounded geometric drawing, followed by the Loop (two interlocking chevron-arrows with rounded terminals).

The palette page (p19) gives only screen values: "RGB 178 252 228, HEX #b2fce4". For print it says to contact Afterpay for the Pantone. That makes the doc self-insufficient for printers.

**WCAG checks:**
| Pair | Ratio | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Black on Mint | 17.94:1 | passes |

White on mint is unusable, and the rules implicitly forbid it: the mint logo is "only ever used on a Black background", and on mint only the black logo appears.

## 5. Depth & material
Flat. The only shapes are the full-radius pill badge and circles (sticker, palette swatches). No shadows; p21 explicitly bans drop shadows, outlines and gradient fills on the logo. Depth comes only from photography.

## 6. Components & patterns
- **Four logo assets:**
  1. Logo lock-up (p14): black on mint is primary; mint on black, black on white and white on black are allowed.
  2. Badge (p15): the lock-up inside a full-radius pill. It is the "primary option" for merchants.
  3. App icon (p16): the Loop in a mint square.
  4. Sticker (p16): the Loop in a mint circle.
- **Partner lock-up (p17).** Afterpay always comes first. Between the logos sits an "X" glyph whose height equals the Afterpay logo height, with a 1 px stroke, rounded caps and lines at 45°. Clearspace around the lock-up equals the X. There are four variants: wordmark or badge, horizontal or vertical. Use the badge when the partner logo has a complex shape.
- **Visual balancing (p18)** pairs the wordmark with 5 differently weighted "Your Brand" sample wordmarks, and the badge with boxed, pill and circular partner marks.
- **Merchant components:**
  - Top strips (mint, black or white bars, one line plus a small logo).
  - Banners: Small Digital 480 × 253 px; Leaderboard 745 × 90 px.
  - Payment-method row: the Loop in a mint chip among card logos.
  - Lightbox explainer with four line icons.
  - PDP and cart "or 4 installments of $X with [badge]" line.
  - Checkout radio option.
  - Instagram post templates (Introduce / Upsell / Educate).
  - Email modules.

## 8. Brand system
**Logo rules**
- **Clearspace (p16)** is drawn on a cyan construction grid. Margin cells equal the wordmark's *x-height*, with the "Height of Logo" bracketed on the right. One exception: clearspace may be relaxed "when sitting alongside text and price breakdown". This is a realistic allowance for PDP lines.
- **Minimum sizes (p16):** logo 64 px / 32 mm, badge 64 px / 32 mm, sticker 32 px / 14 mm, app icon 32 px / 14 mm. The px:mm ratio is not constant (2.0 vs 2.29 px/mm), so treat the mm values as separate print rules, not conversions.
- **Don'ts (p21), 12 tiles with red slash-throughs:**
  - Illegible photo background.
  - Mint logo on images or non-black colours.
  - Sticker on images.
  - Logo in a box (use the badge instead).
  - Wordmark without the Loop.
  - Rotate.
  - Rearrange.
  - Outline.
  - Drop shadow.
  - Recolour or image-fill.
  - Gradient.
  - Distort.
  - Off-palette colours (shown in cyan).
- **Meaning (p13):** the Loop's two arrows link into one continuous shape for the Afterpay–merchant–customer relationship.

**Voice & naming**
- Values (p6–9): Keep it Real, Be Brave, Do the Right Thing, Shape the Future. Purpose (p10): "freedom to get what they want with no downside."
- p11 is a long "We are your customer…" manifesto.
- p23 has the most actionable copy rules:
  - "How to talk about us": customer-centric, budgeting tool, new way to shop, responsible spending.
  - "How not to talk about us": never next to gambling, criminal activity, payday lending, overspending, unmanageable debt, under-18 banking or illegal drugs.
  - Name rule: "Afterpay" only. Never AfterPay, AFTERPAY or afterpay in text, and no all-caps in body. Shown with strike-through examples and green ticks.
- **Message bank (p24–25).** One primary line, "Shop now. Pay later. Always interest-free.", plus four approved alternates, education lines, a price-breakdown formula ("or 4 payments of $XX.XX with [Afterpay Badge] Info"), and social tags.
- **Legal:** a full US disclaimer must appear "as close as possible to the copy". An abbreviated version is allowed when space is tight (p26).

**Imagery**
Warm, sun-lit, candid photography of young, diverse people mid-action (splashing in a pool, running past a coral wall, laughing). It is never codified into rules beyond "choose a photo that has negative space" for text overlay (p26, rule 5).

**Token decisions worth stealing**
- A one-colour brand whose single hue is a pale tint (L≈95%). It works only because it never carries white and always pairs with black (17.9:1).
- The pill badge as the default partner asset, because it carries its own ground on any merchant site.
- Partner lock-up separator specified by stroke, cap and angle.
- Spec slides on #F1F1F1 so that white logo tiles still read as tiles.

## 9. UX
- This is one of the most applicable merchant kits: every integration point (strip, banner, PDP, cart, checkout, lightbox, FAQ, social, email) has a mock-up and the rules are numbered (p26: six rules).
- Merchants are told to keep their own typeface, which avoids font-licence friction.
- **Gaps:**
  - No print colour values.
  - No type scale.
  - Photography is only implied.
  - The values slides put about 16 px white body text directly on busy photos without a scrim, which breaks the deck's own rule 5 in places (p7 over splashing water).

## 10. Craft signals
- The X separator spec (1 px, round caps, 45°, height = logo height) is precise enough to rebuild.
- Clearspace grid uses x-height cells plus a "Height of Logo" bracket in thin cyan lines, consistent across p16–17.
- Spec slides use #F1F1F1 grounds so the #FFFFFF logo tiles in the variant column (p14, p15) read as distinct shapes.
- The Don'ts use one consistent red diagonal through every tile, each with a one-line caption above.
- The naming rule is shown typographically: red strike-through on the wrong forms, green ticks on the right ones.
- Minimum-size px/mm pairs are inconsistent (64 px = 32 mm vs 32 px = 14 mm).
- Proxima Nova is embedded only for the UI mock-ups, so the mock-ups are not set in the brand type.

## 12. Rating
| Axis | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Aesthetics | 7 | The mint-on-black reduction is striking and memorable. The spec slides are tidy but plain, and the values photography is generic lifestyle stock. |
| Originality | 5 | A single-hue fintech brand with values slides and a merchant section is a known format. The Loop and the pill badge are the only distinct assets. |
| Usability | 9 | Gives merchants exact minimum sizes, banner dimensions, a lock-up recipe, an approved message bank, a naming rule, a banned-context list and the legal disclaimer, with every integration point mocked up. |
| Craft | 7 | Consistent template and a precise X spec, but the px/mm minimum sizes are inconsistent, print colour is withheld, there is no type scale, and some text sits over busy photos. |
